Information about iGaming platforms evaluation methodology

We don’t just skim a casino’s homepage and call it a review. Every platform we evaluate gets put through a structured assessment process that covers the things that actually matter to players.

Licensing and regulation comes first. An operator without a valid licence from a recognised authority - the UKGC, MGA, Curaçao eGaming, or equivalent - doesn’t make it past the first filter. Full stop.

Then we look at the chicken road game implementation specifically: is the version they’re running provably fair? What’s the RTP? Are the multiplier caps consistent with what’s advertised? We test this directly rather than taking the operator’s word for it.

Bonus terms get scrutinised properly. Wagering requirements, game contribution percentages, maximum bet restrictions, time limits - all of it. A 200% welcome bonus with a 70x wagering requirement on slots-only is not actually a good bonus, and we’ll say so.

Payment methods, withdrawal speeds, and customer support responsiveness round out the evaluation. We contact support teams with actual questions (not softballs) and document the response time and quality.

Our review process

Step one: identify the platform for review and verify its licensing status through the relevant regulator’s public register.

Step two: create an account as a regular player would, going through the actual registration process and noting any friction points.

Step three: test the chicken road game directly - multiple sessions, varying stake levels, documenting behaviour against published RTP and mechanics.

Step four: test at least one bonus offer end-to-end, reading the full terms and verifying that the actual conditions match what’s advertised.

Step five: contact customer support with a genuine question and document response time and quality.

Step six: test a withdrawal. Withdrawal processing time is one of the most complained-about aspects of online casinos, and we won’t publish a review without having attempted one.

Step seven: write the review. We aim for a draft, an internal review, and at least one revision before publication.

The whole process takes anywhere from a week to several weeks depending on withdrawal speeds. We don’t rush it.
